# Multimeter

[multimtr.com](https://www.multimtr.com/)

<img src="docs/multimeter-logo.svg" width="640" alt="Multimeter: two angular rising-line Ms with lighter rounded lettering in Meter Mint">

A financial instrument for learning how markets move. Choose a stock or cryptocurrency, explore its charts, and choose **Explain this** to understand the measurement on screen.

**[Try your first market experiment](https://www.multimtr.com/)**: choose **Start experiment** to compare a company’s price return over one month and one year. Returning learners can choose **Lessons → Price versus return** to resume.

**[Lead a 15-minute workshop](https://www.multimtr.com/workshop.html)** with the facilitator guide, printable worksheet, discussion prompts, and independent understanding checks.

Open **Explore → Lessons** for the activity library. Build a hypothetical stock mix in **WATCH → Explore charts → Practice portfolio**. Read the [practice portfolio and lessons guide](docs/learning.md). **Explain this** explains the current measurement; **PROBE** answers questions about available figures.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Multimeter is in beta and is a prototype.** Stock data comes from published snapshots and may be delayed.

> [!NOTE]
> **CORR** means correlation, how closely two things move together. **DD** means drawdown, how far something has fallen from its peak.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Nothing here is investment advice.** Multimeter explains measurements. It does not tell you what to buy, sell or hold.

## About the project

I'm Jack McMurry, a student at Emory. I built Multimeter with help from Claude Code and Codex to make market concepts easier to explore through something hands-on.

My process has focused on simplifying technical chart explanations, providing a ready-to-use first activity, and validating historical data before replacing a learner’s selection and observations. Implementation checks cover automated behavior, keyboard navigation, phone layouts, enlarged text, and data recovery.

**Student testing: upcoming.** Passing implementation checks does not establish learning outcomes. The workshop provides a way to observe where students need help, check their explanations independently, and use those observations to guide the next revision.

**Fun fact:** Our signature green has a name: **Meter Mint** (#2EE59D).
