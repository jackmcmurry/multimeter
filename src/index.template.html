    What this is

    A live market meter. Turn the dial to pick a reading (drag it, roll the wheel over it, or use the arrow keys); the tabs on the screen set the chart's range for the coins; press DATA to open the charts behind it. Crypto refreshes every 45 seconds. Index figures refresh about every 15 minutes during US market hours, and the statistics are recomputed from daily closes after each session.

    Dial

    BTC, ETH — spot bitcoin and ether in US dollars, volume-weighted across the exchanges CoinGecko tracks. They trade every calendar day.

    NASDAQ — the Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC), roughly 3,000 listed securities. S&P — the S&P 500 (^GSPC). Both are index levels, not tradable prices.

    STOCK — the stock of the week. A job runs each Monday before the open, scans a fixed 15-name Nasdaq-100 universe, and stores whichever name posted the largest absolute five-session move.

    CRYPTO — the crypto of the week, chosen the same way: the largest absolute seven-day move across a fixed 15-name large-cap universe, bitcoin and ether excluded because they have stops of their own.

    Absolute rather than largest gain, deliberately: ranking by best performer would read as a tip. These report what moved, in either direction — they are not recommendations.

    CORR — bitcoin against the Nasdaq Composite over the last 90 sessions, with beta and R² in the drawer. VOL — bitcoin's realized volatility over 30 sessions, annualized. DD — how far bitcoin sits below its running peak.

    Method

    Correlation and beta use log returns taken after intersecting both instruments on dates each actually traded. Bitcoin trades weekends and the index does not, so without that step BTC's Monday return spans one day while the index's spans three.

    Beta is BTC regressed on the index — the slope of the line through the return scatter. R² is the share of BTC's variance the index explains; a high beta with a low R² is a loose relationship, not a tight one.

    Volatility is the sample standard deviation of the 30-session window, annualized by √252. Drawdown runs on each instrument's own history, since a drawdown belongs to one asset's own path.

    Sources

    CoinGecko's public API for the coins, called directly from your browser, and for the weekly crypto scan. Financial Modeling Prep for the ^IXIC and ^GSPC quotes and daily closes, BTCUSD daily closes and the weekly stock scan; Alpha Vantage for QQQ where a key is configured. Those two need API keys, which cannot ship in a public page, so a scheduled GitHub Action calls them with keys kept as repository secrets and publishes only the results. The market-open lamp is computed in your browser from NYSE hours and holidays.
